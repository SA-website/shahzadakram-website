Fix your internal linking before you write another word of new content. It is the highest-leverage thing you can do to a site over 100 pages, and almost nobody does it properly.

## The conclusion first

If a page matters, something else on your site should say so — with a link, in the body copy, using words a reader would actually click. Most sites don't do this. They rely on the main nav and a sitemap.xml file and call it done. That's not internal linking, that's just navigation.

## Why this works

Search engines still use internal links to understand two things: what a page is about, and how important you think it is relative to everything else. A page with zero incoming internal links is a page you're telling the crawler doesn't matter, no matter what you put in the title tag.

The fix isn't complicated, which is exactly why people skip it — it doesn't feel like "real" work:

1. Pull a full crawl and find orphaned or near-orphaned pages.
2. For every cornerstone page, list the five to ten other pages that should logically point to it.
3. Add the links in-content, not in a "related posts" widget at the bottom nobody scrolls to.
4. Use descriptive anchor text. Not "click here." Not "read more."

## The part people get wrong

They treat this as a one-time audit instead of an editorial habit. Every new page you publish is an opportunity to link backward into your existing library. If you're not doing that at publish time, you're leaving equity on the table on day one.

I don't think there's a clever technical trick hiding here. It's unglamorous, and that's exactly why it still works.
